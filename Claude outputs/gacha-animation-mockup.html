Notes before this goes in

On the ball itself. This is a gachapon capsule rather than a Poké Ball, because a Poké Ball is Nintendo's design and not mine to draw. The choreography you asked for is all here though, and the capsule is arguably the better fit for a tool actually called Gacha. If you want the Poké Ball look, drop your own asset into the container and every animation below drives it unchanged, since the shake and pop are applied to the wrapper rather than the artwork.
The shakes are the whole trick. Each one is a wind-up, a hard swing through, and a settle, with a real pause between. The pause is what creates the suspense: shaking continuously for two seconds reads as loading, three separated shakes read as a decision being made.
Pace matters more than you'd think. The current spin is 1.5 seconds. Full is 2.7, which feels great once but is noticeably longer when eleven people are queued at a table. Quick keeps the three-shake rhythm at 1.9. My pick is Quick for live events and Full only if the queue is short.
It's honest about the result. Same as the current build, the prize is decided and written to the database before any of this plays. The animation just covers the wait, so a slow connection never leaves someone watching a capsule that has already been resolved.
Cheap to run. Pure CSS transforms and opacity, no images and no library, which is what keeps it smooth on the older phones at a meetup. It respects reduced-motion settings too, cutting straight to the reveal.
